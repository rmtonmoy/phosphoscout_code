Generate zero or one novel, testable mechanistic hypothesis linking a specified protein-coding mutation to a disease context.

This function interprets a dense, precomputed mutation-impact payload to propose at most one mechanistic hypothesis that connects a given mutation to disease-relevant biology. It does not re-filter or re-validate inputs; instead, it synthesizes the provided impacted phosphosite windows and associated kinase specificity rewiring events with literature grounding. The workflow includes: basic validation (halt with status='error' if the payload indicates an error), summarization of impacted sites and kinase gain/loss events, interpretation of provided literature (including mutation-specific findings and disease contexts), optional targeted addition of high-signal references, internal generation of up to five candidate hypotheses spanning different sites/kinases/pathways/contexts, strict gating for grounding, completeness, novelty, testability, plausibility, and evidence sufficiency, followed by ranking and selection of a single top candidate. The emitted hypothesis must include explicit inline citations using [ref_id] in all substantive text, a clear, falsifiable causal chain (mutation → site-level phosphorylation prediction → kinase specificity rewiring → named pathway/process → disease-relevant phenotype), concrete predicted observations (including at least one phospho readout), and a laboratory validation plan with site perturbation, kinase perturbation, and rescue/epistasis logic. If no candidate passes all gates, the function returns an empty hypotheses list with status='success' and explanatory notes. If the input payload reports failure, the function returns status='error' and stops.

Args:
    query: str. Free-form input text containing all user-provided information required by this function. This should encode a structured mutation_impact_payload (including its status, inputs for the mutated gene and residue change, variant summary, the list of impacted phosphosite windows with solvent accessibility and significant kinase_events specifying direction as gain/loss, and literature_sources with ref_id-tagged summaries) and may include an optional gene_expression_analyses array from prior evaluations. The expression analyses, if present, are used solely to refine prioritization and context selection; they must not be referenced explicitly in the output.

Returns:
    dict: Dictionary matching the mutation_impact_expert_output schema with keys:
        - "status": str. One of ["success", "error"]. Returns "error" if the provided mutation_impact_payload indicates failure; otherwise "success" even when no hypothesis is emitted.
        - "hypotheses": list[dict]. Zero or one item. Each hypothesis object contains:
            - "hypothesis_id": str. Stable identifier (e.g., "H1").
            - "title": str. One-line hypothesis title; should include inline [ref_id] citations when naming diseases or pathways.
            - "mechanism_signature": str. Compact novelty signature formatted as GENE|MUT|CENTER_POS(S)|KINASE_GAIN/LOSS|DOWNSTREAM_PATHWAY|PHENOTYPE|DISEASE_CONTEXT.
            - "disease_context": list[str]. One or more candidate diseases/cancers where the mechanism may apply; at least one entry must be explicitly literature-supported.
            - "mechanism_summary": str. Two to five sentences that explicitly reference at least one center_pos, at least one kinase, and the direction (gain/loss) as predicted specificity rewiring (not observed phosphorylation), with inline [ref_id] citations after every factual claim (mechanisms, pathways, diseases).
            - "implicated_impacted_sites": list[dict]. One or more impacted sites taken from the input that support the hypothesis. Each site object includes: "wildtype_15mer" (str), "mutant_15mer" (str), "center" (str; S/T/Y), "start_pos" (int), "end_pos" (int), "center_pos" (int), "evidence_info" (str), and a non-empty "kinase_events" list of objects with "kinase" (str), "wildtype_confidence" (number), "mutant_confidence" (number), "delta_metric" (number), and "direction" ("gain" or "loss").
            - "derivation_process": list[str]. Concise, high-level steps describing how the hypothesis was derived; literature-dependent steps must include inline [ref_id] citations.
            - "supporting_references": list[dict]. At least two references (from provided literature and/or newly added). Each includes: "ref_id" (str; unique bibtex-style identifier used for inline citations), "title" (str), "url" (str), "source_type" (str; e.g., web, pubmed, scholar, arxiv, other), "from_provided_literature" (bool), and "relevance_note" (str; 1–2 sentence justification).
            - "predicted_observations": list[str]. At least two concrete predictions, including at least one phospho-specific readout tied to the implicated site(s) and at least one discriminating observation that distinguishes the mechanism from alternatives.
            - "lab_validation": dict. Minimal experimental plan containing: "suggested_models" (list[str]), "assays" (list[str], at least three), "controls_and_confounders" (list[str]), and "feasibility_notes" (str). This object is a sibling of, not a parent to, the fields below.
            - "confidence_level": str. One of ["low", "medium", "high"], reflecting overall confidence based on signal strength, literature support, and mechanistic specificity.
            - "limitations": list[str]. At least one limitation or caveat of the proposed mechanism.
        - "notes": str. Global notes describing missing evidence, assumptions, reasons for emitting no hypothesis, or scope limitations.

Raises:
    ValueError: If the input text cannot be parsed into the expected structures or required fields are missing or malformed.
    RuntimeError: If internal synthesis or validation fails in a way that prevents assembling a schema-conformant result.

Notes:
    - Hard output limit: the function emits at most one hypothesis; if none qualifies, it returns an empty list with status='success'.
    - All substantive statements in title, mechanism_summary, derivation_process, and disease_context must include inline citations using [ref_id]; each cited reference must appear in supporting_references with a unique ref_id.
    - Kinase events represent predicted kinase–substrate specificity rewiring; do not assert increased or decreased phosphorylation as fact. Directional phosphorylation changes may be included only as testable predictions and must be tied to specific phospho readouts in predicted_observations.
    - At least one disease_context entry must be explicitly supported by a reference linking the gene and/or implicated pathway to that disease; additional contexts may be labeled as inference.
    - Novelty and quality gates are strictly enforced. Candidates must: use at least one impacted site and its kinase_event with directionality; provide a complete, falsifiable causal chain; be novel relative to known signatures derived from provided literature; include at least two predictions (one phospho, one discriminating); and be biologically plausible. An evidence sufficiency stop condition returns hypotheses=[] when signals and literature are too weak to support a meaningful, novel, testable mechanism.
    - If the input mutation_impact_payload indicates status!='success', the function returns status='error', hypotheses=[], and explanatory notes without further processing.
    - The lab_validation object must be closed before emitting the sibling fields confidence_level and limitations; these must not be nested inside lab_validation.
